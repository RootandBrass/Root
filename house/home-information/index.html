OLD RUBY REFERENCE RECORD

Home Information

A working record of the physical evidence, known alterations, and current reconstruction of Old Ruby's construction history. Facts and hypotheses are kept separate so later discoveries can refine the story.

Core Information

Construction year: 1914.

Foundation: Large natural stones stacked and mortared/cemented together.

Basement and crawlspace: The basement extends beneath roughly two-thirds of the present house. The remaining roughly one-third is crawlspace.

Original 1914 House

The current reconstruction of the original house is a modest one-bedroom home containing a front porch, living room with chimney, dining room, bathroom, one bedroom with closet, kitchen, and back porch.

The room now used as a bedroom is believed to have been the original kitchen. Evidence includes plumbing remaining in the wall and an old window frame preserved inside the closet.

The bathroom sits between the original bedroom and the room believed to have been the original kitchen. It has clearly been remodeled, possibly more than once. Its general location is believed to be original, but the original fixture arrangement is unknown.

The crawlspace lies beneath the living room, one original bedroom, and bathroom area. The original basement portion lies beneath the dining room and former kitchen/current bedroom area.

Later Addition

Estimated date: Mid-to-late 1920s. This date remains a working estimate and has not yet been documented by a dated historical record.

The addition contains the present kitchen and an additional bedroom. Part of the original back porch was incorporated into the later interior; original porch wood remains visible from the basement beneath the present closet flooring.

The wall between the dining room and current kitchen is the original exterior wall of the 1914 house. It is noticeably thicker than surrounding interior walls.

Other physical evidence for the addition boundary includes a change in floor joists visible from the basement, the surviving old window frame in the closet, and original roof construction preserved in the attic.

The roofline was extended using the same general slope so the addition appears seamless from outside. Inside the attic, the old roof slope at the end of the original house remains in place, including original framing and wood shingles, while the later roof continues beyond it.

Structural Changes

An internal retaining/support wall was added in the basement at an unknown date, estimated broadly between the 1960s and 1990s.

Structural posts have been added, moved, or altered over time, and floor joists were reinforced near the end of the original house where it transitions toward the addition.

Around 2024, the center structural beam and supporting posts beneath the current kitchen, at the far end of the addition, were replaced.

A newer concrete basement floor has covered or removed some earlier physical evidence, including possible traces of the original boiler location, coal storage partitions, footings, or earlier basement access.

Historic Coal Heating Evidence

Old Ruby has surviving physical evidence of an early coal-fired central heating system.

Confirmed evidence: A coal chute survives at the exterior foundation, coal pieces remain in the basement, and some floor joists are blackened. The chimney continues into the basement and has both a round flue connection and a cleanout at its base.

Each room has approximately one two-inch round floor opening near a window. These are believed to be remnants of the former heating system.

Working hypothesis: The evidence is consistent with a coal-fired boiler supplying radiators, and the single floor penetration near the window in each room may be consistent with a one-pipe steam system. No original radiators, valves, or heating pipes are known to survive, so the exact system type is not confirmed.

It is also possible that an exterior basement entrance once served the boiler/coal area, but this has not been confirmed.

Exterior Room Orientation

On the photographed long exterior elevation, reading from right to left: Bedroom 1 is part of the original house; the short window is the bathroom; the large window belongs to Bedroom 2, believed to be the former 1914 kitchen; and the leftmost window belongs to Bedroom 3 in the later addition. The current kitchen continues around the far end of the addition and is not visible on that elevation.

The coal chute enters the basement within the original 1914 portion of the house, near the former-kitchen area.

Working Construction Timeline

1914

Original house built

One-bedroom house with front porch, living room and chimney, dining room, bathroom, kitchen, and back porch. Stone-and-mortar foundation with partial basement and crawlspace.

Early era

Coal-fired central heating

Coal chute, coal remnants, chimney connection, cleanout, and floor penetrations survive as physical evidence. Exact boiler/radiator configuration remains under investigation.

Likely 1920s

Rear addition

House extended beyond its original exterior wall. Present kitchen and additional bedroom added; part of the former back porch incorporated into the enlarged house. Roofline extended to appear continuous.

Later 20th c.

Structural and interior changes

Bathroom remodeling, basement retaining/support wall, post changes, joist reinforcement, heating-system removal/replacement, and other alterations occurred at dates not yet established.

~2024

Kitchen structure reinforced

Center structural beam and supporting posts beneath the current kitchen were replaced.

Evidence Status

Confirmed: Information directly observed in the house or known from completed work.

Physical evidence: Surviving building fabric that supports a reconstruction but may not establish an exact date or original purpose by itself.

Estimated / working hypothesis: A plausible interpretation that should remain open to correction as records or additional physical evidence are found.
