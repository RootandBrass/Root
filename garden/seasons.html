THROUGH THE YEAR

The Seasonal Garden

The garden at Root & Brass is never quite the same from one season to the next. Some plants announce themselves for only a few weeks, while others quietly carry the garden through months of change.

This is a record of when each part of the garden takes its turn to shine.

AWAKENING

Spring

Spring begins quietly. Bulbs emerge, perennials return, flowering trees wake, and the first color appears after a long winter.

Allium

Strong architectural flower heads rise above the returning garden and bring some of the earliest dramatic color.

Primrose

Early flowers bring color close to the ground while much of the perennial garden is still waking.

Crabapple

Spring blossoms make the crabapple one of the garden's most noticeable early-season trees.

Frost Peach

Delicate spring blossom comes before the fruit and provides a brief ornamental season of its own.

Purple & Yellow Lilacs

Fragrant flowers make the lilacs one of the unmistakable signs that the garden has entered spring in earnest.

Lily of the Valley

Small fragrant bells bring detail and softness to shaded parts of the spring garden.

Snow in Summer

Silvery foliage begins building toward the bright carpet of flowers that bridges late spring and early summer.

Hosta

Emerging leaves bring shape, texture, and color back to shaded areas even before flowering begins.

ABUNDANCE

Summer

Summer is the fullest season. Flowers overlap, vines climb, fruit begins forming, and the garden reaches its greatest abundance.

White &nd Red Peony

Large white blooms create one of the garden's most dramatic moments as spring gives way to summer.

Clematis

The two clematis varieties grow together, bringing layered color and vertical interest.

Honeysuckle

Flowers, fragrance, and climbing growth make honeysuckle part of both the ornamental and useful summer garden.

Lupine

Tall flower spikes add vertical structure and a cottage-garden character to the early summer garden.

Lilies

Bold summer flowers rise above surrounding foliage and provide strong midsummer color.

Hollyhock

Tall flowering stalks bring height and an old-fashioned garden character during summer.

Black-Eyed Susan

Warm golden flowers begin carrying the garden toward its later summer palette.

Deep Red Coneflower

Rich red blooms provide strong color through the heat of summer and into the changing season.

Blanket Flower

Warm red, orange, and gold tones keep producing color through much of summer.

Fire Light Hydrangea

Large flower clusters become increasingly dramatic as summer progresses.

Little Lime Punch Hydrangea

Changing flower color provides a long season of interest rather than a single moment of bloom.

Berries & Fruit

Strawberries, raspberries, blackberries, and Frost Peach turn parts of the garden from ornamental spaces into harvest spaces.

THE LAST SHOW

Autumn

Autumn belongs to changing foliage, fading flowers, seed heads, ripening fruit, and the plants that keep their structure after summer begins to disappear.

Fire Light Hydrangea

Flower heads deepen and age beautifully, giving the shrub another season of interest long after its first bloom.

Little Lime Punch Hydrangea

Flowers continue changing color and bring muted autumn tones into the garden.

Black-Eyed Susan

Late flowers and dark seed heads help carry summer color into early autumn.

Deep Red Coneflower

Late blooms eventually give way to seed heads that continue providing texture after flowering.

Blanket Flower

Its warm palette naturally belongs to the transition between summer and autumn.

Crabapple

Fruit and changing foliage give the tree another distinct season after its spring bloom.

Aspen

Golden autumn foliage makes the aspens one of the strongest seasonal features in the garden.

Fruit Garden

The final harvests mark the close of the productive season and begin the transition toward winter.

REST

Winter

Winter strips the garden back to structure. Bark, branches, evergreen foliage, dried seed heads, and snow become the landscape while the perennial garden rests below ground.

Aspen

Pale trunks and fine branching remain visible after the leaves fall, giving the winter garden vertical structure.

Crabapple

The tree's branching form and any lingering fruit continue contributing interest after foliage drops.

Cheyenne Privet

The shrub framework continues defining garden spaces even when active growth has stopped.

Hydrangeas

When left standing, dried flower heads can provide texture and structure against snow.

Coneflower & Black-Eyed Susan

Dried stems and seed heads can remain part of the winter garden rather than disappearing immediately after frost.

The Perennial Garden

Much of the visible garden sleeps, while roots remain protected below the soil preparing for another growing season.
