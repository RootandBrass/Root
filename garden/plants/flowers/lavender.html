--- title: Lavender scientific_name: Lavandula angustifolia plant_group: flowers plant_type: Woody perennial herb bloom: Early to midsummer, with possible repeat bloom after trimming light: Full sun water: Low to moderate; prefers well-drained soil and drying between waterings garden_notes: |- Lavender brings silvery evergreen-to-semi-evergreen foliage, fragrance, and upright purple flower spikes to the garden. It prefers a sunny, well-drained position and is especially useful along paths, borders, and other places where its foliage and scent can be enjoyed. seasonal_record: |- Spring: Silvery foliage begins fresh growth as temperatures warm. Summer: Fragrant purple flower spikes become the main feature and can be harvested as they open. Autumn: The foliage remains attractive after flowering and keeps a tidy, aromatic presence as the garden settles. Winter: Woody stems and silver foliage can continue providing structure where conditions allow. show_harvest: true harvest_season: Early to midsummer, as flower spikes begin opening parts_used: Flower spikes and aromatic leaves harvest: |- Cut flower stems on a dry morning after dew has evaporated. For drying, gather when the lower flowers on the spike have opened but before the entire spike is fully mature. Bundle stems loosely or spread them with good airflow out of direct sun. harvest_uses: Dried bouquets, sachets, culinary seasoning, infused sugar, baking, tea blends, crafts, fragrance, and apothecary preparations. preserve_dry: Bundle small bunches and hang upside down in a dark, dry, well-ventilated place, or dry flower spikes in a single layer. Store when fully dry. preserve_freeze: Fresh flowers can be frozen in water or ice cubes for decorative culinary use, though drying preserves the fragrance better for most uses. preserve_infuse: Flowers can be infused into sugar, honey, vinegar, oil, or other preparations depending on the intended use. preparation: |- - Dry flower stems in small loose bundles away from direct sunlight. - Strip fully dried buds from the stems for jars, sachets, culinary use, or blends. - Use a light hand in cooking; English lavender has a strong floral flavor and can become bitter or perfumelike when overused. show_apothecary: true common_name: English Lavender, True Lavender habitat: |- Lavender is a sun-loving Mediterranean perennial adapted to dry, open conditions. It performs best in lean, sharply drained soil with strong air circulation and dislikes persistently wet roots. identification: |- - Narrow gray-green to silvery leaves arranged along woody stems. - Slender upright flower stalks carrying small violet-purple flowers in dense spikes. - Strong, immediately recognizable aromatic foliage and flowers. - Mature plants form rounded, woody mounds with flowering stems rising above the foliage. benefits: |- Lavender is traditionally valued for its calming fragrance and aromatic qualities. The flowers retain scent well when dried, making them useful in household, culinary, and traditional apothecary preparations. medicinal_uses: |- Lavender has a long tradition of use in aromatic preparations associated with relaxation, rest, and soothing routines. Dried flowers are commonly used in sachets, baths, pillows, steam preparations, and herbal blends. culinary_uses: |- English lavender flowers can be used sparingly in baked goods, shortbread, syrups, sugar, honey, tea blends, lemonade, and savory herb mixtures. The flavor is floral, slightly sweet, and strongly aromatic. magical_uses: |- Lavender is traditionally associated with peace, purification, protection, love, restful sleep, healing, and calm. It is commonly included in sachets, dream pillows, cleansing blends, incense, and home workings intended to create a peaceful atmosphere. personal_notes: |- Lavender connects the garden, kitchen, harvest shelves, and apothecary especially well: the same summer flowers can be enjoyed fresh, dried for winter use, worked into recipes, or kept for aromatic and traditional preparations. sources: |- 1.
