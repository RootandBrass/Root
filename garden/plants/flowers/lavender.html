Upright stalks carry terminal spikes of small purple to violet flowers. The entire plant is strongly aromatic when rubbed. Confirm Lavandula angustifolia before culinary or medicinal use because other lavender species and hybrids differ in chemistry and flavor. benefits: |- Lavender is traditionally valued for its calming fragrance and aromatic household use. Research on lavender preparations suggests possible benefit for anxiety or sleep in some settings, but results depend on the product and route of use. Garden flowers, tea, aromatherapy products, and standardized oral lavender-oil preparations are not interchangeable. medicinal_uses: |- Lavender has a long tradition of use in aromatic preparations for relaxation, mild restlessness, and sleep support, as well as topical preparations for minor skin comfort. Use only correctly identified, unsprayed culinary lavender. A small amount is usually enough. magical_uses: |- Lavender is traditionally associated with peace, purification, protection, love, sleep, healing, and calm. In contemporary magical practice it is often used in sachets, cleansing blends, dream or sleep work, altar arrangements, and household protection. These are spiritual and folkloric associations rather than medical effects. cautions: |- Lavender can cause skin irritation or allergic reactions in some people. Essential oil is highly concentrated and should not be swallowed or applied undiluted. Keep concentrated oils away from the eyes and from young children. Lavender may add to drowsiness when combined with sedating medicines or substances. Safety of medicinal-dose use during pregnancy and breastfeeding is not well established. Culinary quantities of correctly identified lavender are different from concentrated extracts or essential oils. personal_notes: |- Lavender connects the garden, kitchen, harvest shelves, and grimoire especially well: it is beautiful alive, useful dried, strongly fragrant, culinary in small amounts, and rich in traditional symbolism. sources: |- 1.
