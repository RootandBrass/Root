--- title: California Poppy scientific_name: Eschscholzia californica common_name: California Poppy, Golden Poppy plant_group: flowers plant_type: Annual or short-lived perennial wildflower bloom: Spring through summer light: Full sun water: Low once established show_apothecary: true habitat: |+ - Thrives in sunny, open, well-drained ground and tolerates dry, poor soils. - Native to western North America, with a range extending from the Pacific Northwest through California and into southwestern Mexico. - Often self-seeds readily and may naturalize outside cultivation. identification: |+ - **Flowers:** Silky, cup-shaped flowers, usually golden orange to yellow, with four petals. - **Leaves:** Finely divided, blue-green to gray-green, fernlike foliage. - **Growth:** Low to upright branching plants, commonly around 1–2 feet tall depending on conditions. - **Sap:** Plant tissues may exude colored sap when damaged. - Confirm identification before medicinal use; do not assume other poppy species are interchangeable. benefits: |+ - Best known in Western herbal tradition as a gentle calming and sleep-support herb. - Traditionally used for restlessness, nervous tension and difficulty settling into sleep. - Animal studies of California poppy extracts have demonstrated sedative and anxiolytic-like activity, but this does not establish the same effects or an effective dose in humans. - Animal research has also reported peripheral analgesic activity. - Contains biologically active benzylisoquinoline and related alkaloids; these compounds are an important focus of modern California poppy research. medicinal_uses: |+ **Traditional and historical use:** - California poppy has a history of medicinal use among Indigenous peoples of western North America and later Western herbal traditions. - Traditionally used as a calming herb for nervousness, restlessness and sleeplessness. - Historically used for discomfort and pain in some traditional preparations. **Evidence note:** - Laboratory and animal research supports plausible sedative, anxiolytic-like and analgesic activity. - Human clinical evidence is much more limited, so these uses should be recorded as traditional or investigational rather than established medical treatments. culinary_uses: |+ California poppy is kept here primarily as an apothecary and ethnobotanical plant, not as a routine food herb. Do not treat it as interchangeable with culinary poppy seed or other members of the poppy family. magical_uses: |+ **Folklore and modern magical associations:** - Sleep, dreaming and peaceful rest. - Calm, relaxation and easing an unsettled atmosphere. - Associated with sunlight, resilience and renewal because of its bright golden flowers and ability to thrive in dry ground. - Golden/orange flowers may be used symbolically in workings involving optimism, warmth and personal renewal. These are folkloric and modern spiritual associations rather than medical effects. parts_used: Aerial parts, especially leaves and flowering tops, in traditional Western herbal preparations harvest: |+ - Harvest only plants positively identified as *Eschscholzia californica* and growing where collection is permitted. - For an apothecary record, note flowering tops and leafy aerial portions during active bloom. - Avoid plants exposed to herbicides, pesticides, roadside contamination or pet waste. - Leave enough flowers and seed pods for reseeding and pollinators when harvesting from a garden population. preparation: |+ California poppy appears in traditional herbal literature as dried herb, infusion, tincture and commercial standardized preparations. Because its alkaloids are pharmacologically active and preparation strengths vary substantially, this record does not provide a medicinal dose or concentrated-extract recipe. For preservation as a reference herb, dry clean aerial material promptly in a well-ventilated place away from direct sunlight and store fully dried material in a labeled airtight container away from heat, light and moisture. cautions: |+ - California poppy contains pharmacologically active alkaloids and should not be treated as an ordinary culinary herb. - It may have sedating effects. Avoid combining medicinal preparations with alcohol, sedatives, sleep medicines, tranquilizers or other substances that cause drowsiness unless a qualified healthcare professional has reviewed the combination. - Avoid driving or operating machinery if using a preparation that causes drowsiness. - Safety during pregnancy and breastfeeding is not well established; medicinal use is best avoided without professional guidance. - Evidence for medicinal effectiveness and long-term safety in humans remains limited. - California poppy is not opium poppy (*Papaver somniferum*) and should not be assumed to have the same chemistry or effects. Likewise, other poppy species should not be substituted for California poppy. - Confirm plant identity before any internal use. sources: |+ - Becker A, Yamada Y, Sato F.
