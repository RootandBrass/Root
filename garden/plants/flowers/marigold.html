They tolerate heat and short dry periods once established but flower best with regular watering. Deadheading encourages continued bloom until frost. identification: |- Tagetes species are aromatic annuals with divided, fernlike leaves and rounded flower heads in yellow, gold, orange, red, or bicolored forms. Flower shape ranges from simple daisy-like heads to densely doubled pompons. Their foliage has a strong distinctive scent when rubbed. Do not confuse Tagetes marigolds with Calendula officinalis, which is also called pot marigold but is a different genus. benefits: |- Marigolds are valuable companion and pollinator plants. Their flowers contain carotenoid pigments, including lutein in some species and cultivars. Certain Tagetes species have also been studied for effects on plant-parasitic nematodes. Laboratory findings on extracts or essential oils do not establish that homemade marigold remedies treat human disease. medicinal_uses: |- Different Tagetes species have histories of traditional use for digestive, skin, and inflammatory complaints in several cultures. These uses vary by species and preparation, and modern human evidence is limited. Root & Brass records these traditions without treating garden marigold as a substitute for medical care. culinary_uses: |- Some correctly identified Tagetes species and cultivars are used as edible flowers or flavorings. Petals may be used sparingly as a colorful garnish or seasoning when the exact plant is known to be food-suitable and has not been treated with non-food-safe pesticides. Tagetes should never be substituted automatically for Calendula in a recipe. magical_uses: |- In modern folk-magic and garden traditions, marigold is commonly associated with protection, solar energy, courage, remembrance, prosperity, and honoring the dead. Its bright gold and orange flowers are often used symbolically in altar work, seasonal arrangements, protective charms, and remembrance practices. These associations are cultural and spiritual traditions rather than medical properties. cautions: |- Tagetes is not interchangeable with Calendula officinalis. Sap, flowers, and especially concentrated essential oils may irritate sensitive skin or trigger contact dermatitis. Some Tagetes extracts and essential oils can be phototoxic, so concentrated preparations should not be placed on skin before sun exposure. People sensitive to plants in the Asteraceae family should use extra caution. Do not ingest essential oil, and do not use medicinal-dose preparations during pregnancy, breastfeeding, childhood, or with medications without qualified guidance. personal_notes: |- Marigolds are grown in the Root & Brass garden every year for their long bloom season, vivid color, pollinator value, harvestable petals, and strong place in seasonal and magical garden traditions. sources: |- 1.
