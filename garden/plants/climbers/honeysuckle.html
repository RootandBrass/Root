It grows in full sun to partial shade with evenly moist, well-drained soil, often flowering best when its roots stay cool and its upper growth reaches the light. identification: A twining vine—not a bush honeysuckle—with opposite, oval to elliptic leaves and terminal clusters of long, two-lipped tubular flowers. Buds may be pink or reddish before opening creamy white to yellow, and the flowers become strongly fragrant in the evening. Lonicera periclymenum can form glossy red berries, but the Root & Brass vine has not produced berries. Confirm this species rather than relying on the common name "honeysuckle," which is shared by many Lonicera species with different chemistry and safety. benefits: The fragrant flowers provide nectar for moths, bees, and other pollinators and bring a calming sensory presence to the evening garden. Laboratory research has identified antioxidant compounds in Lonicera periclymenum, but this does not establish a safe dose or prove that a homemade preparation treats disease. medicinal_uses: Leaves and flowers of Lonicera periclymenum have been recorded in folk practice for coughs and respiratory complaints. Much of the better-known medicinal literature on "honeysuckle" concerns a different species, Lonicera japonica, used in traditional Chinese medicine. Those uses and preparations must not be transferred to this garden vine. Root & Brass records the history but does not recommend homemade internal remedies from this plant. culinary_uses: No household culinary use is recommended in this record. The Root & Brass vine has not produced berries; if berries ever develop, they are ornamental and should not be eaten. Although some references describe carefully identified honeysuckle flowers as edible, species confusion is common and does not make the rest of the plant safe. harvest: For a pressed specimen or fragrant display, gather a small cluster of newly opened, unsprayed flowers after dew has dried. Leave plenty for pollinators and avoid diseased, moldy, roadside, or chemically treated material. Do not harvest berries for food, syrup, tincture, or medicine. preparation: Enjoy the flowers on the living vine, press a specimen for the journal, or air-dry a small amount for a non-ingestible botanical display. Keep dried material clearly labeled and separate from culinary herbs. Do not make honeysuckle tea, infused honey, syrup, tincture, oil, or salve from this plant for medicinal use. cautions: This Root & Brass plant is a honeysuckle vine and has not produced berries. If Lonicera periclymenum berries ever develop, they are ornamental and not to be eaten; ingestion can cause gastrointestinal illness. Keep berries and harvested material away from children and pets. Do not assume that a preparation made from Lonicera japonica or another medicinal honeysuckle is suitable for this species. Avoid internal medicinal use during pregnancy, breastfeeding, childhood, or alongside medication because safe dosing and interactions are not established. If any part is swallowed and symptoms develop, contact Poison Control in the United States at 1-800-222-1222; call emergency services for collapse, seizure, trouble breathing, or inability to wake. This record is educational and is not medical advice. personal_notes: The Root & Brass specimen is a twining honeysuckle vine, not a bush honeysuckle.
