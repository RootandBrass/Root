Its astringency comes largely from tannins. Blackberry leaf is not appropriate for serious gastrointestinal illness, and laboratory antioxidant or antimicrobial activity does not prove that homemade preparations treat disease. culinary_uses: Fully ripe berries may be eaten fresh or used in preserves, sauces, baked goods, and syrups. Cook and preserve fruit with tested food-safety methods. harvest: |- - Gather only healthy, unsprayed young leaves without stripping or weakening fruiting canes. - Wear gloves and long sleeves around prickled cultivars. - Pick berries only when fully colored and ripe; wash gently and refrigerate promptly. preparation: |- - **Leaf infusion:** Dry leaves fully, steep for a mild tea, and strain carefully. - **Gargle:** Cool an infusion completely and spit it out after use if it is intended only as a rinse. - **Storage:** Label dried leaves separately from raspberry leaf and keep them dry and airtight. cautions: |- - Do not self-treat diarrhea accompanied by blood, black stool, high fever, severe pain, dehydration, recent antibiotic use, or prolonged symptoms. - Tannin-rich preparations may upset the stomach and could affect absorption of medicines or nutrients; avoid taking them at the same time as prescription drugs without professional advice. - Medicinal-use safety during pregnancy, breastfeeding, and childhood is not well established. sources: |- 1.
