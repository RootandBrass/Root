Peppermint evidence applies to *Mentha × piperita*, not automatically to every mint. Identify the plant before medicinal use and specifically rule out pennyroyal (*Mentha pulegium*). benefits: |- - **Digestive tradition:** Peppermint leaf preparations are traditionally used for indigestion and flatulence. - **Clinical distinction:** Enteric-coated peppermint oil has evidence for reducing some irritable-bowel symptoms, but a tested capsule is not equivalent to garden tea or homemade essential oil. - **Everyday value:** Correctly identified culinary mints make pleasant aromatic teas and can help make hydration more appealing. medicinal_uses: |- Once the garden species is confirmed, the leaves may be recorded for traditional digestive use. Peppermint tea is generally milder than concentrated oil and is commonly used after meals for flavor and comfort. No medicinal claim should be transferred to this plant solely because it smells minty. Species identity and preparation strength matter. culinary_uses: Fresh leaves can flavor tea, water, fruit, salads, sauces, desserts, and savory dishes. Culinary use is the safest starting point after the species has been positively identified. harvest: |- - Snip clean stems before or as flowering begins, when the foliage is strongly aromatic. - Harvest above a leaf node to encourage branching. - Use only unsprayed growth and inspect leaves carefully for mint rust or mildew. preparation: |- - **Leaf infusion:** Bruise a few clean leaves and steep in hot water for a mild culinary tea. - **Drying:** Hang small bundles or spread leaves in a thin layer away from direct sun; store only when crisp. - **Essential oil:** Distilled oil is highly concentrated. Do not improvise internal dosing or apply it undiluted. cautions: |- - Confirm the species. Pennyroyal oil can cause severe liver injury, organ failure, pregnancy loss, and death; it must not be treated as ordinary garden mint. - Peppermint may worsen reflux or heartburn. People with gallstones or other biliary disorders should seek medical guidance before medicinal use. - Never apply peppermint oil or menthol preparations to the face of an infant or young child; inhaled menthol can interfere with breathing. - Medicinal-dose safety during pregnancy and breastfeeding is uncertain.
