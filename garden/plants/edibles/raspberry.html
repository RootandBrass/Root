A cooled infusion has been used as a gargle for minor mouth or throat irritation, while oral preparations have a traditional role in mild, short-lived diarrhea and menstrual spasm. Raspberry leaf should not be used to delay evaluation of severe pain, heavy bleeding, dehydration, persistent diarrhea, or infection. culinary_uses: Fully ripe berries are nutritious foods rich in fiber and polyphenols. This entry keeps ordinary fruit consumption separate from medicinal leaf preparations. harvest: |- - Gather healthy, unsprayed leaves before fruiting or from non-fruiting lateral growth without stripping a cane. - Wear gloves around prickles and avoid leaves marked by rust, mildew, or chemical spray. - Dry leaves quickly in a thin layer with strong airflow. preparation: |- - **Infusion:** Use fully dried leaf for a mild tea or cooled gargle; strain fine leaf hairs carefully. - **Storage:** Keep dried leaf airtight, labeled, and away from light and moisture. - Do not assume a pregnancy tea blend is appropriate merely because raspberry leaf is traditional. cautions: |- - Despite its reputation as a pregnancy herb, EMA assessment states that safety data are insufficient and medicinal use during pregnancy is not recommended without obstetric guidance. - Seek medical care for diarrhea with blood, black stool, fever, severe pain, dehydration, or symptoms lasting more than a short period. - Ask a clinician before medicinal use while breastfeeding, for children, or with chronic disease or prescription medicines. sources: |- 1.
