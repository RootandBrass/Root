PRESERVATION WORKBENCH

Preserving the Harvest

Choose the method for the plant and the way it will eventually be used. Start with correctly identified, clean, unsprayed material and label every batch before it leaves the worktable.

DryingFreezingInfusingGarden Reference

01

LOW MOISTURE · LONGER KEEPING

Drying

Best for aromatic herbs, sturdy leaves, and flowers that will be used in teas, blends, crafts, or carefully prepared topical products.

Air Dry

  1. Harvest after the dew has dried.
  2. Remove damaged material and surface moisture.
  3. Tie small bundles or spread flowers in one layer.
  4. Keep in a dark, dry, well-ventilated place.

Use a Dehydrator

  1. Arrange material in one layer.
  2. Use low herb-friendly temperatures.
  3. Check frequently rather than relying on one fixed time.
  4. Cool completely before packing.

Ready when: Leaves crumble, stems snap, petals feel papery, and no cool or soft center remains.

Store: Airtight, labeled, and away from heat, moisture, and light.

02

FRESH COLOR · QUICK STORAGE

Freezing

Useful for ripe fruit and herbs whose flavor matters more than their thawed texture.

Fruit

  1. Use sound, ripe fruit.
  2. Wash gently and dry well.
  3. Tray-freeze pieces before transferring to freezer containers when loose pieces are useful.
  4. Label and freeze promptly.

Herbs

  1. Clean and dry thoroughly.
  2. Freeze whole or chopped in practical portions.
  3. Herbs can also be frozen in water cubes for cooking or drinks.
  4. Expect thawed herbs to be soft.

03

FLAVOR · FRAGRANCE · EXTRACTION

Infusing

An infusion moves a plant's flavor, aroma, color, or soluble compounds into another medium. Storage rules depend on the liquid and intended use.

Water & Tea

Use positively identified edible plants and refrigerate leftovers promptly.

Vinegar

Use commercial vinegar of known acidity and clean plant material; keep untested homemade preparations refrigerated.

Syrup

Fresh garden syrups are perishable unless prepared and processed with a tested recipe.

Oil

Use thoroughly dried plant material for shelf-life experiments and follow tested food-safety guidance for edible oils.

Important: “Infused” does not automatically mean edible, medicinal, or shelf-stable. Keep topical preparations clearly marked for external use.
