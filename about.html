ABOUT ROOT & BRASS

About

Root & Brass is a personal collection of old-house history, garden notes, projects, discoveries, and the things that gather meaning when you live with them long enough.

The Place

At the center of Root & Brass is 301 Ruby, a 1914 home with more than a century of history still tucked into its walls, details, and surrounding ground.

The Garden

The garden is an ongoing experiment in growing, learning the seasons, keeping what thrives, and trying again when nature has other plans.

The Journal

The journal is where the pieces come together: stories, research, projects, observations, discoveries, and whatever seems worth keeping.

Why Root & Brass

Roots and brass may seem like unlikely companions. One grows quietly beneath the earth while the other is forged by fire and human hands. But the more I considered them, the more alike they became.

Roots are strength without spectacle.

They begin small and reach outward, searching for what will sustain them. They anchor themselves deeply enough to withstand wind, drought, bitter winters, and changing seasons. They nourish what can be seen while doing most of their work where no one is looking.

A single root can only reach so far. Together, roots form networks. They share the ground, hold the soil, support growth, and connect one generation of life to the next.

There is something deeply familiar in that.

Family has roots. So does a home. We put down roots in the places we choose to care for, and those places eventually become part of us. Roots are ancestry and memory, but they are also persistence. They remind us that surviving a difficult season is sometimes less about standing rigidly against it and more about holding fast beneath the surface until it is time to grow again.

Brass carries a different kind of strength.

It is made, not grown. Copper and zinc are brought together to create something stronger and more useful than either would be in quite the same way alone. It can be worked, shaped, polished, repaired, and passed from one generation to another.

Brass conducts.

It carries energy from one place to another, making connections possible. Like roots beneath the soil, its usefulness often lies in the connections we do not see.

And brass ages honestly.

Left untouched, it changes. It darkens and develops a patina. Time leaves evidence on its surface, but age does not make it less beautiful or less valuable. Often, it makes it more interesting. You can polish away those years and reveal the shine beneath, or you can leave the marks of time exactly where they are.

There is beauty in both.

Root & Brass lives somewhere between those two ideas.

The things we build with our hands and the things that grow beyond our control. Strength and flexibility. Nature and craftsmanship. History and change.

It is about family and the networks we create around ourselves. It is about hard work that may never be noticed, but matters nonetheless. It is about caring for a home, tending a garden, preserving a story, repairing what can be repaired, and recognizing when the marks left by time deserve to remain.

It is about resilience without pretending that resilience leaves us unchanged.

Roots grow around obstacles.

Brass carries the evidence of every hand, every season, and every year that has touched it.

Both endure.

Both connect.

And both become more meaningful with time.

That is Root & Brass.
