## Six cards and their positions

| Position | Card | Orientation | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Wife | The Magician | Reversed | Feeling powerless, unheard, or exhausted from trying to change the marriage. |
| 2. Husband | Nine of Swords | Upright | Possible private worry, anxiety, guilt, or distress. This does not establish remorse or his actual thoughts. |
| 3. The Connection | The Empress | Reversed | Emotional depletion, neglected tenderness, or an imbalance in giving and receiving care. |
| 4. Strength | The Fool | Upright | Space for a fresh start, a different approach, or renewed openness. |
| 5. Challenge | Eight of Cups | Upright | Emotional withdrawal or the search for deeper fulfillment; not necessarily a literal departure or divorce. |
| 6. Direction | The Tower | Reversed | Resisting or delaying necessary change, possibly by avoiding conversations that must happen. |

## Overall reading

The cards suggest a relationship shaped by exhaustion and unspoken worries. The wife's Magician reversed can reflect the feeling that her efforts are not changing anything. The husband's Nine of Swords may symbolize private distress, but the cards cannot tell us whether he feels guilt or remorse. Between them, the Empress reversed suggests that closeness and care have become strained.

The Fool offers a potential strength: the willingness to begin again without repeating the exact same patterns. The Eight of Cups asks whether someone has withdrawn emotionally or is seeking a more nourishing connection. The Tower reversed cautions that change postponed may remain a source of tension.

**Reflection:** What needs to be spoken openly, what care is missing, and what would a genuinely new start require from *both* partners?

*Tarot is interpreted here as a tool for reflection, not evidence about another person's private feelings or a prediction of future events.*
