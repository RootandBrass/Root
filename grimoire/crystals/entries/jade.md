geology: |+
  - **Jade** refers to two different minerals: jadeite and nephrite
  - Jadeite is a pyroxene mineral, while nephrite is a dense interlocking form of amphibole
  - Both are exceptionally tough and have long been carved into tools, ornaments, jewelry, and ceremonial objects
  - Natural jade occurs in green, white, lavender, yellow, brown, black, and other shades
  - Jadeite has a Mohs hardness of approximately **6.5 to 7**; nephrite is approximately **6 to 6.5**
  - Some commercial jade is dyed, bleached, polymer-treated, or imitated, so gentle care is safest

associations: |+

  - Connected with the **heart chakra**, especially in green varieties
  - Often regarded as a stone of good fortune and balanced judgment
  - Associated with family harmony, enduring friendship, and peaceful strength
  - Has deep cultural significance in many traditions, especially across East and Central Asia and Mesoamerica

care: |+
  - Clean gently with a **soft cloth and mild soapy water**
  - Rinse briefly and dry thoroughly
  - Cleanse energetically with **moonlight, smoke, sound, or selenite**
  - Avoid harsh chemicals, acids, bleach, and abrasive cleaners
  - Avoid steam and ultrasonic cleaning unless the stone is professionally confirmed untreated
  - Keep away from prolonged heat, which can affect treated jade
  - Protect from hard blows despite jade's exceptional toughness
  - Store separately to preserve its polished surface
