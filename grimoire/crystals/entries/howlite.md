geology: |+
  - A calcium borosilicate hydroxide mineral
  - Usually white or cream with irregular gray, brown, or black veining
  - Naturally forms in nodules and cauliflower-like masses
  - Has a Mohs hardness of approximately **3.5**, making it relatively soft and easy to scratch
  - Its porous surface accepts dye readily, so blue-dyed howlite is often sold as imitation turquoise
  - Natural howlite is generally opaque with a matte to softly polished appearance

care: |+
  - Clean with a **soft, dry or barely damp cloth**
  - Avoid soaking because howlite is porous
  - Avoid saltwater, acids, household chemicals, perfumes, and oils
  - Do not use steam or ultrasonic cleaners
  - Keep dyed pieces away from water and cleaners that may affect their color
  - Cleanse energetically with **smoke, sound, moonlight, or selenite**
  - Protect from scratches, knocks, and falls because howlite is relatively soft
  - Store separately from harder stones
