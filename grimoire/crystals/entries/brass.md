---
title: Brass
variety: Copper–Zinc Alloy
color: Warm Yellow-Gold to Reddish Gold
geology: |+
  - Brass is a **metal alloy made primarily of copper and zinc**, not a crystal or naturally occurring mineral.
  - Its color and properties vary with the proportions of copper and zinc and with any other added metals.
  - It has been shaped into tools, fittings, vessels, jewelry, and decorative objects.
  - The surface may darken or develop a patina over time. A plated or lacquered object needs different care from solid, unfinished brass.

associations: |+
  - In personal symbolic work, brass can stand for **warmth, endurance, craftsmanship, and the beauty of useful things**.
  - Its golden appearance makes it a fitting symbol for abundance, confidence, and welcome.
  - Because it is made by bringing metals together, it can represent connection and work built to last.
  - These are symbolic interpretations, not mineral properties or universal correspondences.

uses: |+
  - Use a brass object as a focus for an intention about steady work, home, or prosperity.
  - Keep a small piece near a writing desk, altar, or meaningful household object as a reminder of what you are building.
  - Pair it with a note, candle, or other ritual object if that suits your practice.
  - Record the story of the particular brass piece when you know its origin.

care: |+
  - Dust with a soft, dry cloth and keep the object dry after handling.
  - Before polishing, check whether the piece is solid brass, plated, or lacquered; polishing can remove a valued finish or patina.
  - Avoid abrasive scrubbing and prolonged exposure to moisture.
  - For an older or historically significant piece, preserve its existing surface and ask a conservator before cleaning aggressively.
  - For symbolic cleansing, use sound, smoke at a safe distance, or a quiet intention without wetting the metal.

pairings: Citrine, Pyrite, Tiger's Eye, Clear Quartz
