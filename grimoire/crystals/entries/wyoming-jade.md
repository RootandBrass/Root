geology: |-
  - **Wyoming Jade** is nephrite, one of the two mineral materials properly called jade; the other is jadeite
  - Nephrite is a dense, interlocking aggregate of amphibole-group minerals in the tremolite–actinolite series
  - Wyoming deposits occur within ancient granitic gneisses and are commonly associated with amphibolites, shear zones, faults, and folds
  - Colors range from white and pale apple green through olive and deep green to nearly black
  - Has a Mohs hardness of approximately **6–6.5**, but its tightly interwoven fibers give it exceptional toughness and resistance to breakage
  - Wyoming adopted nephrite jade as its official state gemstone in **1967**
  - Natural pieces may show weathered rind, dark inclusions, cloudy zones, or variations in translucency

care: |-

  - Although nephrite is exceptionally tough, protect polished surfaces from abrasive materials
  - Store separately from harder stones such as quartz to prevent surface scratches
