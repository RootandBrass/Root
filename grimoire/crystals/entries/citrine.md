variety: Citrine

geology: |+
  - A yellow to orange variety of **quartz**, composed primarily of silicon dioxide
  - Its color is produced by iron-related color centers within the crystal structure
  - Natural citrine is relatively uncommon and is often pale yellow, smoky yellow, or golden
  - Much of the deep orange citrine sold commercially is **heat-treated amethyst**
  - Has a Mohs hardness of **7**
  - May occur as transparent crystals, clusters, tumbled stones, or polished points

associations: |+
  - Traditionally associated with **abundance, confidence, joy, creativity, and personal power**
  - Connected with the **solar-plexus and sacral chakras**
  - Often called a merchant's stone because of its association with prosperity and successful enterprise
  - Associated with optimism, motivation, and the courage to act
  - Frequently used in manifestation and goal-setting practices

uses: |+
  - Encourages confidence and a positive outlook
  - Traditionally used for prosperity, success, and opportunity
  - Supports motivation, creativity, and decisive action
  - Useful for money work, business intentions, and long-term goals
  - Encourages healthy self-worth and personal authority
  - Can be kept near a workspace, cash box, planner, or vision board
  - Supports breaking large goals into practical steps
  - Often used to bring warmth and uplifting energy into a room
  - Helpful for manifestation practices grounded in planning and consistent effort

care: |+
  - Clean gently with a **soft, damp cloth**
  - Briefly rinse with cool or lukewarm water, then dry thoroughly
  - Cleanse energetically with **smoke, sound, moonlight, or selenite**
  - Avoid prolonged strong sunlight because the color may fade
  - Avoid sudden temperature changes, which may aggravate internal fractures
  - Avoid harsh chemicals, abrasive cleaners, and prolonged soaking
  - Protect points and clusters from knocks or falls
  - Store separately from softer stones because quartz can scratch them

pairings: Pyrite, Jade, Green Aventurine, Carnelian, Clear Quartz
notes: |+
  Natural citrine and heat-treated amethyst are both commonly sold as citrine. Their appearance can help with identification, but origin or treatment may require confirmation from a knowledgeable seller or gemologist.
