geology: |+
  - An opaque hydrated copper-aluminum phosphate mineral
  - Its blue color is associated with copper, while iron may contribute greener tones
  - Often forms in veins or nodules within dry, copper-rich regions
  - Has a Mohs hardness of approximately **5 to 6**
  - Natural matrix may appear as brown, black, or gray webbing
  - Much commercial Turquoise is stabilized, dyed, reconstructed, or imitated, so provenance matters

associations: |+
  - Traditionally associated with **protection, travel, truth, friendship, and good fortune**
  - Connected with the **throat chakra**
  - Long valued as an amulet by cultures across several continents
  - Often used to encourage honest communication and safe journeys
  - Associated with wisdom gained through experience

uses: |+
  - Traditionally carried for protection during travel

  - Can be carried during travel, study, or major transitions
  - Supports speaking from experience without becoming overly blunt
  - Often kept as a symbol of good fortune and well-being

care: |+
  - Clean only with a **soft, dry or barely damp cloth**
  - Avoid soaking because Turquoise is porous
  - Avoid saltwater, acids, household chemicals, oils, perfumes, cosmetics, and perspiration
  - Do not use steam or ultrasonic cleaners
  - Cleanse energetically with **smoke, sound, moonlight, or selenite**
  - Avoid prolonged sunlight and heat, which may alter color or affect treatments
  - Protect from scratches, pressure, knocks, and falls
  - Store separately from harder stones
  - Treat unknown or stabilized specimens with extra care
