geology: |-
  - A naturally occurring **volcanic glass** formed when silica-rich lava cools rapidly with minimal crystal growth
  - The white or gray "snowflakes" are clusters of **cristobalite**, a crystalline form of silica, that develop within the black obsidian
  - Typically black with scattered pale gray or white radial patterns
  - Has a Mohs hardness of approximately **5 to 5.5**
  - Breaks with a sharp conchoidal fracture and should be handled carefully around chipped edges
associations: |-
  - Traditionally associated with **grounding, balance, protection, self-reflection, and releasing old patterns**
  - Often used symbolically for finding calm and clarity within difficult or chaotic circumstances
  - Connected with the **root chakra** for grounding and the **third-eye chakra** for self-awareness
  - The contrast of black and white is commonly associated with balance, integration, and seeing multiple sides of a situation
uses: |-
  - Used in grounding and centering practices
  - Supports reflection during shadow work and journaling
  - Traditionally carried for protection and energetic boundaries
  - Encourages recognition of habits or patterns that may need to change
  - Useful during meditation when seeking emotional steadiness and perspective
  - Can be placed near a workspace or altar as a reminder of balance and discernment
care: |-
  - Wipe gently with a **soft, damp cloth** and dry thoroughly
  - A brief rinse in lukewarm water is generally suitable, but avoid prolonged soaking
  - Cleanse energetically with **moonlight, smoke, sound, or selenite**
  - Avoid harsh chemicals and abrasive cleaners
  - Protect from hard impacts because obsidian can chip and form very sharp edges
  - Store separately from harder stones that may scratch its polished surface
pairings: "Smoky Quartz, Hematite, Black Tourmaline, Clear Quartz, Selenite"
