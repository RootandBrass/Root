  - **Picasso Jasper** is a lapidary trade name rather than a formally defined mineral species
  - Material sold under this name is commonly described as patterned marble or metamorphosed limestone, although some sellers apply the name to jasper-like, silica-rich material
  - Green pieces typically display olive, sage, cream, grey, or black areas crossed by abstract lines and angular veining
  - The dark patterns are often attributed to iron- or manganese-rich minerals deposited along fractures
  - Hardness can vary significantly with composition: carbonate-rich Picasso Stone is much softer than true quartz-based jasper
  - Exact identification requires testing because appearance and trade names alone cannot reliably distinguish marble-rich material from silica-rich material
