---
title: "Frost Peach"
common_name: "Frost Peach"
scientific_name: "Prunus persica 'Frost'"
plant_type: tree
found_location: "Root & Brass garden"
habitat: |-
  A cold-hardy deciduous fruit tree grown in full sun with well-drained soil and consistent moisture while fruit develops. This cultivated peach belongs in the food-first side of the apothecary rather than the home-remedy cabinet.
identification: |-
  A small deciduous tree with long, narrow, finely toothed leaves and pink spring blossoms. The summer fruit is round and softly fuzzy, ripening to yellow flesh beneath blushed skin. Confirm the garden label and fruit before use; this record is specifically for the cultivated Frost peach.
benefits: |-
  The ripe peach flesh is a nourishing seasonal food. It provides water, dietary fiber, vitamin C, potassium, and naturally occurring carotenoid and polyphenol plant compounds. These nutrients support an overall varied diet, but the fruit should not be presented as a treatment or cure for disease.
medicinal_uses: |-
  Peach flowers, leaves, bark, and kernels appear in historical and traditional materia medica. Those uses are recorded here for ethnobotanical context only: reliable human evidence is limited, and the tree's non-fruit tissues can contain cyanogenic compounds. Root & Brass does not recommend homemade peach-leaf, bark, twig, pit, or kernel remedies.
culinary_uses: |-
  Use ripe, sound flesh fresh or cooked. Peaches are suited to baking, sauces, preserves, drying, and freezing. Remove and discard the pit before serving or preserving. For shelf-stable canning, follow a current tested recipe rather than improvising processing times or acidification.
parts_used: |-
  Ripe fruit flesh only for household use. Do not use the pit or kernel, leaves, bark, twigs, or wilted plant material as food, tea, tincture, syrup, or medicine.
harvest: |-
  Harvest when the green background color has turned creamy yellow, the fruit smells fragrant, yields slightly near the stem, and releases with a gentle twist. Pick into a shallow basket and handle carefully to prevent bruising. Leave damaged, moldy, or fermented fruit out of preparations.
preparation: |-
  Wash under cool running water, cut around the stone, twist the halves apart, and remove the pit. Eat promptly, refrigerate, dry, cook, or freeze using a tested food-preservation method. See the Root & Brass Harvest & Use guide for the garden's drying, freezing, and infusing framework; peach is listed here for fruit preparations, not medicinal infusions from leaves or bark.
cautions: |-
  Peach pits and kernels, leaves, bark, and twigs can contain cyanogenic glycosides that may release toxic cyanide. Never chew or eat the kernel inside a cracked pit, and do not make tea, tincture, syrup, extract, or "vitamin B17"/amygdalin remedies from these tissues. Keep pits and prunings away from children, pets, and livestock. Peach can also cause food allergy or pollen-related oral allergy symptoms; stop eating it and seek urgent help for trouble breathing, throat swelling, faintness, or a rapidly spreading reaction. Whole or broken pits are a choking hazard. This record is educational and is not medical advice.
