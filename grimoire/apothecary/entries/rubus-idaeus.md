---
title: Raspberry
common_name: Red Raspberry, Raspberry Leaf
scientific_name: Rubus idaeus
plant_type: shrub
found_location: Root & Brass garden
habitat: |-
  - **Garden setting:** A perennial fruiting bramble grown in full sun.
  - **Soil:** Performs best with well-drained soil and consistent moisture during flowering and fruit development.
  - **Growth:** Biennial canes emerge from a perennial crown and require regular thinning and support.
identification: |-
  - **Canes:** Upright to arching, usually with small prickles; new and fruiting canes differ in color and texture.
  - **Leaves:** Compound leaves with toothed margins and a distinctly pale or silvery underside.
  - **Fruit:** Aggregate red berries that pull away from the receptacle, leaving a hollow center.
  - **Look-alikes:** Confirm the bramble by leaves, canes, flowers, and hollow-centered ripe fruit before harvesting medicinal leaf.
benefits: |-
  - **Recognized traditional use:** Raspberry leaf is recorded for minor menstrual cramps, mild mouth or throat inflammation, and mild diarrhea.
  - **Astringent action:** Leaf tannins help explain its traditional use as a gargle or short-term digestive tea.
  - **Evidence limit:** These uses are based on longstanding traditional experience rather than strong clinical trials.
medicinal_uses: |-
  Dried raspberry leaf may be recorded as a traditional astringent herb. A cooled infusion has been used as a gargle for minor mouth or throat irritation, while oral preparations have a traditional role in mild, short-lived diarrhea and menstrual spasm.

  Raspberry leaf should not be used to delay evaluation of severe pain, heavy bleeding, dehydration, persistent diarrhea, or infection.
culinary_uses: |-
  Fully ripe berries are nutritious foods rich in fiber and polyphenols. This entry keeps ordinary fruit consumption separate from medicinal leaf preparations.
parts_used: Leaves; ripe fruit as food
harvest: |-
  - Gather healthy, unsprayed leaves before fruiting or from non-fruiting lateral growth without stripping a cane.
  - Wear gloves around prickles and avoid leaves marked by rust, mildew, or chemical spray.
  - Dry leaves quickly in a thin layer with strong airflow.
preparation: |-
  - **Infusion:** Use fully dried leaf for a mild tea or cooled gargle; strain fine leaf hairs carefully.
  - **Storage:** Keep dried leaf airtight, labeled, and away from light and moisture.
  - Do not assume a pregnancy tea blend is appropriate merely because raspberry leaf is traditional.
cautions: |-
  - Despite its reputation as a pregnancy herb, EMA assessment states that safety data are insufficient and medicinal use during pregnancy is not recommended without obstetric guidance.
  - Seek medical care for diarrhea with blood, black stool, fever, severe pain, dehydration, or symptoms lasting more than a short period.
  - Ask a clinician before medicinal use while breastfeeding, for children, or with chronic disease or prescription medicines.
