---
title: Blackberry
common_name: Bramble, Blackberry Leaf
scientific_name: Rubus fruticosus
plant_type: shrub
found_location: Root & Brass garden
habitat: |-
  - **Garden setting:** A perennial fruiting bramble that produces best in full sun.
  - **Soil:** Prefers fertile, well-drained soil with steady moisture while fruit develops.
  - **Growth:** Long, thorny or thornless canes require support and annual pruning.
identification: |-
  - **Canes:** Arching brambles that may root where their tips touch soil; many cultivars carry stout prickles.
  - **Leaves:** Compound leaves, usually with three to five toothed leaflets.
  - **Fruit:** Dark purple-black aggregate berries that retain their pale receptacle when picked, unlike hollow raspberries.
  - **Confirmation:** Identify the plant across an entire season before gathering leaves; ornamental brambles and wild hybrids can be confusing.
benefits: |-
  - **Traditional astringent:** Tannin-rich leaves have a long record of use for mild diarrhea and as a gargle for minor mouth or throat irritation.
  - **Food value:** Ripe fruit supplies fiber, vitamin C, and anthocyanin pigments as part of a varied diet.
  - **Evidence limit:** Most medicinal-leaf evidence is traditional, laboratory, or preclinical; good human trials are limited.
medicinal_uses: |-
  A mild dried-leaf infusion has been used traditionally for short-lived digestive upset and as a cooled mouth or throat rinse. Its astringency comes largely from tannins.

  Blackberry leaf is not appropriate for serious gastrointestinal illness, and laboratory antioxidant or antimicrobial activity does not prove that homemade preparations treat disease.
culinary_uses: |-
  Fully ripe berries may be eaten fresh or used in preserves, sauces, baked goods, and syrups. Cook and preserve fruit with tested food-safety methods.
parts_used: Leaves; ripe fruit as food
harvest: |-
  - Gather only healthy, unsprayed young leaves without stripping or weakening fruiting canes.
  - Wear gloves and long sleeves around prickled cultivars.
  - Pick berries only when fully colored and ripe; wash gently and refrigerate promptly.
preparation: |-
  - **Leaf infusion:** Dry leaves fully, steep for a mild tea, and strain carefully.
  - **Gargle:** Cool an infusion completely and spit it out after use if it is intended only as a rinse.
  - **Storage:** Label dried leaves separately from raspberry leaf and keep them dry and airtight.
cautions: |-
  - Do not self-treat diarrhea accompanied by blood, black stool, high fever, severe pain, dehydration, recent antibiotic use, or prolonged symptoms.
  - Tannin-rich preparations may upset the stomach and could affect absorption of medicines or nutrients; avoid taking them at the same time as prescription drugs without professional advice.
  - Medicinal-use safety during pregnancy, breastfeeding, and childhood is not well established.
