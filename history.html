KEMMERER, WYOMING

Madame Isabelle

Among the more complicated stories in Kemmerer’s past is that of Madame Isabelle Burns, a woman associated with the town’s historic red-light district during an era shaped by mining, railroads, transient labor, and rapid western growth.

Like many western mining communities, Kemmerer developed an economy that extended far beyond coal. Saloons, boarding houses, gambling, bootlegging, and prostitution became part of the social landscape surrounding the men who came west to work.

Women working within that world are often reduced to a sentence or a label in traditional histories. Their lives, however, were far more complicated.

The story of Madame Isabelle offers a way to examine Kemmerer from another angle: through the lives of women, the hidden economy of a mining town, and the parts of community history that were often remembered privately but rarely preserved with the same care as mines, businesses, and prominent civic leaders.
